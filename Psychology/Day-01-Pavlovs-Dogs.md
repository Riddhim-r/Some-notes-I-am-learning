## The dog who drooled too early: classical conditioning

*About a 5-minute read.*

---

**St Petersburg, 1890s.** Ivan Pavlov isn't trying to discover anything about the mind. He's studying digestion, and he does it by measuring how much the dogs in his lab drool when food arrives.

But the dogs keep ruining his data. They start drooling *before* the food comes: when they hear the lab assistant's footsteps, or the clink of the food bowl. Most scientists would have called it a nuisance and thrown out those measurements. Pavlov got curious instead.

So he tested it. He sounded a buzzer or a ticking metronome, and then gave the dog food. Buzzer, food. Buzzer, food. After enough repeats, he sounded the buzzer and gave **no food at all.** The dog drooled anyway.

(Fun fact: the famous "bell" is mostly a myth. Pavlov mainly used buzzers and metronomes. And his Nobel Prize in 1904 was for digestion, not for this.)

## The visual

| | Before | During training | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pavlov's dog** | Buzzer → no drool | Buzzer + food → drool | Buzzer alone → **drool** |
| **You and your phone** | Random beep → nothing | Buzz + message → excitement | Buzz alone → **excitement** |

## What this means

Your brain links together things that keep happening together. Once the link forms, the signal alone triggers the feeling. This is called **classical conditioning**.

The same thing happens to you. Your phone's buzz was once just a sound. Then it came with messages, likes and good news, again and again. Now the buzz alone gives you a little jolt, even before you've seen anything. Ever felt your phone vibrate when it didn't? That's the conditioning firing on its own.

**Did it hold up?** Yes, it's rock-solid. It's one of the most replicated findings in all of psychology, and it's still used to treat phobias today.

## Try it today

- **On yourself:** for one day, every time you hear your notification sound, notice the jolt before you look. Count how many times you reach for your phone *because of the sound*, rather than because you decided to.
- **With others:** while you're sitting with 2–3 friends, quietly play a common notification sound (WhatsApp's, for example) from a video. Count how many of them glance at their own phones. Then tell them what you just did. You've just watched conditioning happen.
